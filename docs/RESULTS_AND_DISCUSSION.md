## 1. Introduction and Experimental Context

Automated factual verification of abstractive summarization models remains a critical challenge, particularly in multilingual and non-English settings where cross-lingual transfer, syntactic divergence, and low-resource tokenization introduce compounding failure modes. This study investigates the central research question:

> **Can factuality-aware prompt engineering improve the factual consistency of LLM-generated summaries, and which Natural Language Inference (NLI) verification architecture provides the most reliable diagnostic resolution across English and Hindi?**

To address this question empirically, a controlled $2 \times 3 \times 2$ factorial benchmark was conducted across:
1. **Two Generation Prompt Strategies:**
   - **Prompt A (Standard Baseline):** Standard task-oriented abstractive summarization instructions requesting concise summaries that preserve main information.
   - **Prompt B (Factuality-Aware Constrained):** Factuality-constrained instructions explicitly directing the model to extract and summarize *only* facts directly supported by the text, prohibiting external knowledge and speculative resolution of ambiguities.
2. **Three Verification Architectures:**
   - **Whole-Document NLI:** The complete source document acts as premise and the entire generated summary acts as hypothesis in a single cross-encoder pass.
   - **Sentence-Level NLI:** The full source document acts as premise for each individual summary sentence independently.
   - **Hybrid Claim-Level Verification:** Summary sentences are decomposed into atomic factual claims via a deterministic rule-based extractor. For each atomic claim, top-$K$ ($K=3$) evidence passages are retrieved from the source article using Okapi BM25, and evaluated via mDeBERTa cross-encoder inference.
3. **Two Typologically Distinct Languages:** English (Indo-European, Germanic) and Hindi (Indo-European, Indo-Aryan).

All evaluations were executed deterministically on frozen summaries generated by Cohere's Command A+ (`command-a-plus-05-2026`) from BBC XL-Sum articles. Exactly 20 frozen summaries (10 English, 10 Hindi; 5 Prompt A, 5 Prompt B per language) were subjected to each of the three verification architectures, yielding exactly 60 strategy-level evaluation outputs. All 60 evaluations succeeded with zero runtime failures and zero quota interruptions.

**Deterministic Aggregation Hierarchy:**
- **Level 1 (Passage-to-Claim Grounding):** A claim $c$ evaluated against retrieved passages $\{p_1, p_2, p_3\}$ is classified as:
  $$\text{label}(c) = \begin{cases}
  \text{'entailment'}, & \text{if } \max_{k} P(\text{entailment} \mid p_k, c) \ge 0.50 \\
  \text{'contradiction'}, & \text{if } \max_{k} P(\text{contradiction} \mid p_k, c) \ge 0.50 \text{ and } \max_{k} P(\text{entailment}) < 0.50 \\
  \text{'neutral'}, & \text{otherwise}
  \end{cases}$$
- **Level 2 (Summary Aggregation):** Summary ratios represent the proportion of constituent sentences (or claims) assigned to each respective label.

### 2.2 English-Specific Findings

1. **Whole-Document Verification (C1 vs. C2):**
   - In C1 (Prompt A), all 5 English documents evaluated to `neutral` (macro entailment ratio = 0.0000; mean entailment score = 0.0547).
   - In C2 (Prompt B), 2 out of 5 English documents evaluated to `entailment` (macro entailment ratio = 0.4000; mean entailment score = 0.4549). This represents an absolute gain of +0.4000 in macro entailment ratio and an eight-fold increase in mean cross-encoder entailment score.
2. **Sentence-Level Verification (C3 vs. C4):**
   - C3 evaluated 54 sentences across 5 summaries: 31 entailed (57.41% micro), 3 contradicted (5.56% micro), 20 neutral (37.04% micro). Macro entailment ratio was 0.5440, and macro contradiction ratio was 0.0654.
   - C4 evaluated 39 sentences across 5 summaries: 21 entailed (53.85% micro), 3 contradicted (7.69% micro), 15 neutral (38.46% micro). Macro entailment ratio was 0.4250, and macro contradiction ratio was 0.0625.
   - Sentences in Prompt B were on average fewer in number (39 vs. 54, a 27.8% reduction), reflecting tighter prompt constraints.
3. **Hybrid Claim-Level Verification (C5 vs. C6):**
   - In C5 (Prompt A), 63 atomic claims were extracted and evaluated against 177 retrieved passages. Macro entailment ratio was 0.5518 (micro: 57.14%), with a macro contradiction ratio of 0.0154 (1 single contradicted claim).
   - In C6 (Prompt B), 43 atomic claims were extracted and evaluated against 116 retrieved passages. Macro entailment ratio was 0.4809 (micro: 51.16%), with a macro contradiction ratio of 0.0533 (2 contradicted claims).

### 2.5 Verification Strategy Comparison

The empirical data highlights stark operational differences among the three verification paradigms:

1. **Whole-Document NLI:**
   - Characterized by acute **neutral collapse**. Across all 20 whole-document evaluations (C1 + C2), 16 evaluated to `neutral` (80.0%), 4 evaluated to `entailment` (20.0%), and 0 evaluated to `contradiction` (0.0%).
   - The average cross-encoder entailment score was only 0.1639 in C1 and 0.3546 in C2. The cross-encoder is incapable of isolating localized contradictions when masked by hundreds of uncontradicted document tokens.
2. **Sentence-Level NLI:**
   - Yielded the highest entailment sensitivity of all strategies, averaging macro entailment ratios of **0.6015 (C3)** and **0.6138 (C4)**.
   - Preserves complete document context by pairing the full source article with each individual sentence, allowing cross-attention to resolve long-range coreference and diffuse background facts.
   - However, it exhibited the highest computational cost across all runs (averaging 14,870 ms in C3 and 16,392 ms in C4 per summary).
3. **Hybrid Claim-Level Verification:**
   - Achieved high diagnostic precision by isolating discrete factual assertions into standalone claims (averaging 8.4 claims per summary in C5 and 10.0 claims in C6).
   - Produced macro entailment ratios of **0.5792 in C5** and **0.4264 in C6**, with macro contradiction ratios of **0.0610 in C5** and **0.0410 in C6**.
   - Drastically improved execution efficiency: average latency per summary was **6,895 ms in C5** (a 53.6% reduction compared to sentence-level) and **3,645 ms in C6** (a 77.8% reduction compared to sentence-level).

## 3. Discussion

### 3.1 Prompt B Impact: Does Factuality Prompting Improve Reliability?

The empirical findings offer mixed, nuanced evidence regarding Hypothesis 1:
- **Overall Effect:** At the aggregate level across both languages, Prompt B reduced the overall macro contradiction ratio in both fine-grained verification strategies (sentence-level: 8.38% to 5.88%; hybrid: 6.10% to 4.10%).
- **Subgroup Divergence:** However, this reduction was primarily driven by the Hindi subset (where sentence-level contradiction decreased from 10.22% to 5.50%, and hybrid contradiction plunged from 10.67% to 2.86%). For English, sentence-level contradiction was virtually unchanged (6.54% vs. 6.25%), while hybrid verification recorded a higher contradiction ratio under Prompt B (5.33% vs. 1.54%).
- **Absence of Universal Superiority:** Consequently, the benchmark does not demonstrate that Prompt B universally or consistently improves factual reliability across every language and verification architecture. 
- **Epistemic Compression Mechanism:** Where Prompt B did suppress contradictions, the primary operational mechanism appears to be an epistemic compression rather than an increase in verified entailment. By discouraging ungrounded details, Prompt B induced shorter and more conservative summaries, which often elevated the neutral ratio (e.g., English sentence-level neutral ratio rising from 39.06% to 51.25%).
- **Sample Limitations:** Given the small sample size ($N=5$ summaries per subgroup), these observations must be treated as preliminary exploratory findings warranting larger-scale replication, rather than definitive proof of factuality enhancement.

### 3.2 The Whole-Document Neutral Collapse Mechanism

Whole-document verification produced overwhelming neutral rates (90.0% in C1 and 70.0% in C2). This phenomenon—termed **neutral collapse**—stems directly from transformer cross-attention dynamics over disparate sequence lengths:
1. When a 1,500-token source article is paired with a 100-token summary paragraph, the cross-encoder computes self-attention across the full joint sequence.
2. In bidirectional attention, the majority of premise tokens describe background narrative that is entirely absent from the summary.
3. Because the standard NLI task penalizes predicting `entailment` when hypotheses contain details unmentioned in the premise, the cross-encoder conversely treats excessive premise surplus as non-entailment. Lacking explicit mutual contradiction, the softmax layer defaults to the `neutral` class.
4. Consequently, whole-document NLI fails as an operational factuality filter.

### 3.3 Sentence-Level Verification: Localized Premise-Hypothesis Alignment

Sentence-level verification circumvents neutral collapse by isolating one summary proposition at a time. Evaluating individual sentences against the full article premise lifted macro entailment from 0.1000–0.3000 (whole-document) to **0.6015 (C3)** and **0.6138 (C4)**.
- **Advantage:** Full article context remains accessible to the cross-encoder, allowing it to aggregate evidence dispersed across non-contiguous paragraphs and resolve pronominal references.
- **Disadvantage:** Computational redundancy. For a summary with 5 sentences, the entire multi-thousand-token document is encoded 5 separate times. On local CPU hardware, this incurred an average latency of 14.8 to 16.4 seconds per summary, rendering it computationally prohibitive for large-scale production pipelines.

### 3.4 Hybrid Claim-Level Verification: Strengths and Retrieval Bottlenecks

The hybrid architecture combines deterministic rule-based atomic claim extraction with BM25 passage retrieval. By restricting the cross-encoder's premise to the top-3 most relevant 250–500 character passages, the hybrid approach achieved:
- **Fine-Grained Error Attribution:** Isolated discrete contradicted claims (e.g., numerical discrepancies, erroneous causal claims) at the sub-sentence level.
- **Computational Efficiency:** Cut average inference latency by **53.6% in Prompt A** (6,895 ms vs. 14,870 ms) and **77.8% in Prompt B** (3,645 ms vs. 16,392 ms) compared to sentence-level verification.

However, the hybrid strategy introduces a new vulnerability: **retrieval error propagation**. If BM25 fails to retrieve the exact passage containing the supporting evidence (due to lexical mismatch or syntactic paraphrasing), mDeBERTa is forced to evaluate the claim against incomplete context. As observed in Case 2 (`world-europe-55173830`), when an atomic claim retains multiple conjoined predicates and BM25 returns a passage supporting only one, the cross-encoder may penalize the missing context as a contradiction.

### 3.5 Cross-Lingual Discrepancies and the Generation Scratchpad Phenomenon

A detailed qualitative inspection of the machine-readable outputs revealed an unexpected generative behavior in the Hindi benchmark subset:

#### Observed Evidence
1. **Planning Text / Scratchpad Output:** In several Hindi Prompt B summaries (notably records `international-50448073`, `060528_soldiers_desert`, and `international-40883742`), the generator (`command-a-plus-05-2026`) emitted English planning or scratchpad-like text alongside or in place of pure Devanagari Hindi summaries (e.g., `"The task: generate a concise summary using only information supported by the article... Let's extract key points: - British Defence Ministry denies..."`).
2. **Evaluation Metrics Divergence in C6 vs. C4:**
   - Under Hybrid Claim-Level verification (C6), these Hindi Prompt B summaries exhibited a macro neutral ratio of **59.96%** and a micro neutral ratio of **78.95%** (45 out of 57 claims were neutral), with a macro entailment ratio of only **37.18%**.
   - Under Sentence-Level verification (C4), the exact same frozen summaries evaluated against the exact same source documents achieved a macro entailment ratio of **80.25%** and a macro neutral ratio of only **14.25%**.

#### Proposed Explanation (Hypothesis)
We propose the following hypothesis to explain this sharp divergence between verification architectures on identical summaries:
- **Lexical Overlap Bottleneck in BM25:** The extracted atomic claims from the scratchpad sections were phrased in English, whereas the source news articles in the benchmark repository were in Devanagari Hindi. Because BM25 relies on exact lexical token matching, English query terms have little to no lexical overlap with Devanagari script passages.
- **Degraded Retrieval Relevance:** This lexical mismatch can substantially degrade BM25 retrieval relevance, causing the retriever to return poorly matched evidence passages.
- **Neutral Predictions from Poorly Matched Evidence:** When the mDeBERTa cross-encoder evaluates an English claim against poorly matched Devanagari passages, it cannot establish semantic grounding and appropriately predicts `neutral` (unsupported), driving the high neutral ratio observed in C6.
- **Cross-Lingual Attention in Sentence-Level NLI:** In contrast, Sentence-Level NLI (C4) provides the entire source document to mDeBERTa. Because mDeBERTa-v3 was pre-trained on multilingual parallel corpora (including XNLI and Hindi data), its full-context cross-attention layers can align the semantic content of English sentences directly with Hindi source paragraphs without relying on lexical token matches.

*Distinction Between Observation and Explanation:* The presence of English scratchpad text and the contrasting neutral/entailment distributions across C6 and C4 are directly documented empirical observations. The role of BM25 lexical mismatch as the primary driver of this disparity is a compelling and theoretically sound explanatory hypothesis, but the pilot experiment did not isolate a dedicated dense-retrieval ablation to formally prove causation.

## 4. Research Limitations and Epistemic Boundaries

To maintain scientific integrity, the findings presented in this report must be interpreted strictly within the following epistemic boundaries:

1. **Small Sample Size:** The controlled experiment evaluated exactly 20 frozen summaries across 6 conditions (60 strategy-level evaluations). Each condition comprises 5 English and 5 Hindi summaries. These results represent an **exploratory pilot benchmark**, not a statistically powered clinical trial.
2. **Absence of Statistical Generalizability:** Because $N=5$ per sub-condition, differences in percentage points are indicative of trends rather than proven statistical significance. P-values and confidence intervals are deliberately omitted to avoid over-claiming.
3. **Uncalibrated Model Scores:** Cross-encoder output probabilities ($P(\text{entailment}), P(\text{contradiction})$) from `mDeBERTa-v3-base-xnli` reflect internal softmax distribution values over pre-trained features. They are **uncalibrated heuristic confidence scores**, not true Bayesian posterior probabilities of factual truth in the physical world.
4. **Lack of Human Gold Annotations:** Claims and summaries were evaluated exclusively through automated sequence-classification models. No calibrated human fact-checker annotations were collected for this run to establish absolute ground-truth accuracy.
5. **Deterministic Heuristic Boundaries of Claim Extraction:** The rule-based claim extractor splits on coordinated independent clauses, subordinating conjunctions, and punctuation. While it successfully avoids noun-phrase over-splitting in both English and Hindi, it does not resolve anaphoric pronouns, cross-sentence coreference, or elliptical omissions.
6. **Heuristic Aggregation Threshold ($0.50$):** The Level-1 decision policy assigns `entailment` or `contradiction` based on $\max_{k} P \ge 0.50$ across retrieved passages. This threshold is an engineering design choice adopted from standard classification conventions, not an empirically optimized decision boundary.
7. **Cross-Lingual Performance Disparity:** Multilingual cross-encoders exhibit variable calibration across typologically distinct scripts (Latin vs. Devanagari) due to uneven pretraining corpus representation.
8. **Strict Separation of Historical Runs:** All results presented herein were generated exclusively with the pinned local model `MoritzLaurer/mDeBERTa-v3-base-xnli-multilingual-nli-2mil7` (revision `b5113eb`). Historical hosted Cohere NLI evaluations remain isolated in legacy directories and must not be conflated with these local benchmarks.
9. **No Claims of Absolute Superiority:** The data does not establish that hybrid claim-level verification is universally superior to sentence-level verification. Rather, it reveals a specific trade-off: hybrid verification offers superior computational throughput and claim-level granularity, but incurs vulnerability to passage retrieval failures.

## 5. Conclusion and Recommendations

The controlled benchmark provides exploratory pilot evidence regarding factuality-aware prompting and multi-granularity verification:
- **Prompt Strategy Effect:** Factuality-aware prompting (Prompt B) reduced overall macro contradiction ratios in both fine-grained verification strategies (from 8.38% to 5.88% in sentence-level, and from 6.10% to 4.10% in hybrid claim-level). However, this effect varied across languages and verification architectures: the reductions were primarily driven by the Hindi subset (where sentence-level contradiction dropped from 10.22% to 5.50%, and hybrid contradiction fell from 10.67% to 2.86%), whereas English hybrid verification recorded a higher contradiction ratio under Prompt B (5.33% vs. 1.54%). Prompt B should not be characterized as universally improving factuality across all subgroups.
- **Verification Strategy Trade-Offs:**
  - **Whole-Document NLI** proved uninformative for diagnostic factuality filtering due to persistent neutral collapse (70%–90% neutral).
  - **Sentence-Level NLI** exhibited the highest entailment sensitivity (60%–61%) and resilient cross-lingual semantic alignment, but at high computational cost on CPU (14.8–16.4 seconds per summary).
  - **Hybrid Claim-Level Verification** enabled granular proposition-level evaluation and a 2× to 4.5× speedup over sentence-level verification, but exhibited sensitivity to passage retrieval failures when queries and documents differed lexically or linguistically.
- **Future Directions:** To address the observed retrieval bottleneck when generation outputs contain cross-lingual planning text, future multilingual factuality frameworks should explore integrating multilingual dense retrieval or applying strict generation output constraints.
